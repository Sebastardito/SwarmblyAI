# Results — R13 confirmatory run: does the anchored wiki beat a simple memory?

**11 October 2026 · pre-registration `PREREGISTRATION_BENCH_R1X_EN.md`,
Section 2, with Amendments 7–12 (before running) and 15 (review
declarations, after) · commit `5d0edea` with a clean tree, digests checked
against `lce_validation/data/model_digests.json`, exclusive Ollama server ·
Python 3.9.6 (deviation from the locked 3.11; Amendment 15.1) · 251
questions**

Raw outputs in `results/bench_r13_confirm/` (`results.json`, with every
answer; `wikis_cache/`, the 18 digested wikis). The figures in this
document come from `analysis.json`, regenerated by
`python -m lce_validation.r13_analysis --dir bench_r13_confirm`; they are
checked by `run_derive` and `tests/test_doc_figures.py`. This document
replaces the same-day version that stated the wiki "wins forgetting" and
that the global death therefore "does not fire"; Section 4 explains why.

## 1. Design

251 paired questions: simple 100, cross-document 30, updated 31,
contradiction 30, deleted 30 and unanswerable 30 (Amendment 8). Six users:
the five own synthetic users (v1–v4) and a frozen LongMemEval subset (MIT
licence, 8 questions). The 3 N1 questions were removed before running.
Five arms with the same 200-token context budget: A0 no memory, A1 first
200 words of the sources, A2 raw RAG (BM25 over sentences; Amendment 15.9),
A3 LCE wiki (3 digesters × 3 responders, full crossed design) and A4 wiki
+ Γ (diagonal). Digestion through the native seeded endpoint with the
tolerant parser (Amendments 7 and 10); answers at temperature 0.

## 4. Reading

**The `deleted` type does not measure forgetting.** Amendment 6 (b)
declared this before this run (N2) and the confirmatory run keeps the
defect: no arm ever saw the deleted source, and A0, with no memory,
abstains on 100 % of those questions. A3's +0.115 over A2 on `deleted`
measures that the wiki retrieves less related information than RAG, which
is the same coverage loss seen from the other side (the abstention table
shows it next to accuracy). Forgetting is measured in F-forget
(`RESULTS_2026-10-10_Fforget_AA_EN.md`).

**The global death.** The Spanish text of the pre-registration, which is
the one the provenance hashes, says «la wiki no gana en 3 ni 5» (the wiki
wins on neither type 3 nor type 5); the English text says "does not win on
3 and 5", which reads as "does not win on both" (Amendment 15.3: the two
versions diverge). By the letter of the Spanish text the rule does not
fire, but only because of type 5, which is invalid as a forgetting
measure. By the English text, it fires. Without the N2 artefact, raw RAG
beats the wiki overall and the wiki does not win on updates, which is the
situation for which the pre-registration foresees "the design is
simplified (publishable result)". The simplification in the next
paragraph rests on R13-H1 and on the measured cause (coverage), not on
the rule having fired.

**What is retired and what remains.** The wiki's justification by
question-answering accuracy is retired: on this material, with 2–3 B
models and a 200-token budget, retrieving raw sentences beats retrieving
digested claims, and the gap is explained by what digestion loses. What
the wiki provides and RAG lacks by construction is measured elsewhere:
verifiable forgetting of a source (F-forget, mechanism) and traceability
by anchoring. Whether those properties justify the cost of digestion is a
design decision, not a result of this run. Simplifying the design means
that default retrieval should be over the sources, with the wiki as a
governance layer (provenance, forgetting, training) rather than the main
answering context.

2. A2 uses BM25 over sentences, not embeddings (Amendment 15.9).
3. The first version of this document and of the analyser treated type 5
