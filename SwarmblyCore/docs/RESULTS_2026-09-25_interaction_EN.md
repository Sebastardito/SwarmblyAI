# Confirmation results — H-INT does not survive

**25 September 2026 · executes `PREREGISTRATION_2026-09-25_interaction_EN.md`**

Corpus: `swarmbly_ref/data/benchmark.jsonl`, sha256 `d94bb9cc7694d0f7…`,
verified against the pre-registration before running. 123 comparable cells, 20
tasks, 5 models.

---

## 1. The result, without hedging

**H-INT is dead, and it died for the exact reason the pre-registration
anticipated.**

| test | ρ | p | H-INT predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary — LOO strength vs tax** | **−0.078** | 0.192 | ρ > 0 |
| Length control (§4.2) | −0.370 | 0.009 | ρ > 0 |
| Ceiling control (§4.3) | −0.075 | 0.211 | ρ > 0 |
| Both controls | −0.374 | 0.007 | ρ > 0 |

The primary test gives nothing, and where something appears the sign is the
opposite of the one predicted. By the death condition in §6, **the finding is
withdrawn**.

---

## 2. Why it appeared: the statistic invents it on its own

The pre-registration (§3) warned that `tax = 1 − frag/mono` carries `mono` in
the denominator, and that correlating it with the `mono` of the same cell
produces association by construction. The measurement confirms this three ways:

| | ρ |
|---|---|
| Spearman(`mono` of **the same cell**, tax) | **+0.675** |
| Spearman(**independent LOO strength**, tax) | **−0.078** |
| **Simulation: `frag` and `mono` drawn independently** | **+0.684** |

The last row is the decisive test. On data where **by construction no relation
exists**, the statistic returns +0.684 — practically identical to the +0.675
observed. **The "interaction" is generated by the formula, not by the data.**

And the threshold sweep fails its declared death condition: the sign of the
strong stratum changes within the interquartile range (−7.2 % with the cut at
0.446; +8.3 % with the cut at 0.673). There are not two regimes; there is a
gradient manufactured by the denominator.

**The stratified table is not published.**

---

## 3. How the error was produced

The finding was found by **exploring** the data, and was presented as settled
before it was confirmed: in its first reading it was described as "the
finding the context unlocked", it was claimed to resolve an open objection, and
it was said to change the project's statement.

It does not. It is the **fifth appearance** of this project's recurring defect —
*a check that claims more than it measured* — and it appeared in the very
document that reproached that defect in other measurements. That recurrence is
informative: the defect is not produced by carelessness, it is produced by the
structure of the analysis, and that is why the defence has to be structural.

What worked was the pre-registration: writing down the suspected defect and the
design that breaks it **before** running. With the threshold chosen by eye and
the coupled statistic, the analysis would have confirmed the error with large
numbers and an interval that excluded zero.

---

## 4. What does survive: the length confound

The same suspicion was applied to the other open claim — that the aggregate tax
is confounded with output length. **That one withstands all three tests.**

| | ρ | reading |
|---|---|---|
| Spearman(length ratio, tax) | −0.575 | the original observation |
| Artifact floor (tax permuted) | **+0.125** | what the shape of the statistic produces on its own: small and of opposite sign |
| **Spearman(ratio, `frag − mono`)** — no denominator | **+0.595** | same magnitude, clean statistic |
| **Spearman(words_frag, score_frag) WITHIN task** | **median +0.683**, positive in **16 of 18 tasks** | direct test |

The last row settles the matter: **within a single task, writing more scores
more**, in 16 of 18 tasks. The fragmented arm writes 1.39× more (median), and
that is enough to explain much of its advantage.

A contrast worth keeping in view: with the clean statistic, length gives +0.595
and node strength gives **+0.068**. One is an effect; the other is noise.

---

## 5. The real state of the evidence, after confirmation

**Unaffected** (and it is most of it):

- **T07R** — the controlled M4 experiment: constant ρ, both arms fragmented, δ
  varied by cut quality. The length criticism never applies here: both arms
  write on the same budget. δ rises in 32/32, the tax worsens in 19/32. **It
  remains the best test in the set and it remains negative for M4.**
- **T04R** — 127 intra-category cells, ρ = −0.13. It converges with the
  independent measurement over `tables24` (ρ = −0.456).
- **T05R** ρ scaling, **T06R** corpus instrument, **T02R** triage, **T0R**
  router against the truth, **T0LR** L per class, **T10R** aligner.
- **T0RR** routability: still FAIL (AUC 0.38–0.47).

**Affected:**

- **T09R** — the aggregate tax is confounded with output length. The −18.32 % is
  not interpretable as it stands, and **there is no interaction that rescues
  it**: the one that was proposed is withdrawn by this very document.

---

## 6. What can be claimed today

> The instrument works and is self-verified. M4 is falsified in two independent
> instruments, with a controlled experiment at constant ρ. The router is refuted
> at the cell level. The ρ scaling law is verified against real data.
> **Feasibility cannot be claimed yet: the measured advantage of fragmenting is
> confounded with the output budget, and until that is matched the criterion has
> neither been met nor failed — it has not been measured.**

That is less triumphant than "criterion met, −18 %", and it is what the data
support. It is also publishable: an instrument of one's own and verified, two
falsified models, a router refuted per cell and a verified scaling law are a
result, even though none of the four is the one that was expected.

---

## 7. What is missing, and it is cheap

A run with **matched output budget** between arms: the monolithic arm receives
`max_tokens = T`, the fragmented arm receives `T` divided among its fragments.
With that, the measured tax answers to fragmentation and not to verbosity.

The command is `run_benchmark.py --matched`. With resume-by-key it costs minutes
of compute, not hours.
