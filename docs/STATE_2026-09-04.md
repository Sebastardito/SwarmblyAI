## 5. Instrument defects found, and what they had in common

Twelve, documented in `REVISION_2026-08-12.md`, **all one-sided toward the
project's own hypothesis**. That is the signature of confirmation bias when the
person measuring and the person proposing are the same, and it is why ADR-001
exists.

Since then, five more, and the pattern moved twice:

1. **The chain misplan** (`INCIDENT_2026-09-04_chain_misplan.md`) — the first
   defect in the **stimulus** rather than the instrument. A `then` in an
   output-format directive planned every enumerated prompt as a dependency
   chain. Nothing in the harness was watching the stimulus; every gate added
   that week watches the measurement.
2. **The segmenter's question/data split** — the first found by V7's oracle arm,
   and the first that is an architectural limitation rather than a bug.
3. **`run_fragmented` kept no copy of its own output** — an arm asymmetry not in
   the metric but in *what the row keeps*. An independent scorer could see the
   monolithic arm and not the fragmented one.
4. **V7's instructions asked for ids its own evaluator could not parse** — a key
   whose identifiers were not the ones the prompt asked the model to emit.
5. **The `smoke` tier still instructed an invocation the runner refuses** —
   `tables-final --tau <T>`, fixed in `tables-dev` on 3 September and left
   standing in the tier an operator runs *first*, because the test written for
   that defect was anchored on the two functions where it had been fixed. It
   also told the reader to redefine a ρ grid that had been redefined that
   morning. Found by running `smoke` and reading what it printed.

Three of the first four are asymmetries between arms, in three different places:
the stimulus, the partition, and the record. The fifth is a different class
again — a **stale instruction**: true when written, never re-checked, and
repeated to someone who then acts on it. The two documents that kept saying a
token was embedded in a remote URL are the same class. Neither is a wrong
measurement, which is why nothing was watching for them.

## 7. Open, and not fixable by any run

- **Duplication versus ρ.** The segmenter repair of 4 September covers the case
  where each question names one part of the material and **refuses** the case
  where two questions need the same part. That refusal exists because
  duplicating a unit inflates `sum(|task_i|)` above `|P|` and raises the
  reachable ρ floor. But the **oracle partition duplicates freely** — it hands
  each claim exactly the facts it requires, and those sets overlap. So the
  partition that is viable in principle is a *covering*, not a partition, and ρ
  charges for every duplicated token. The architecture's low-ρ pitch and its own
  oracle are in direct tension and no segmenter resolves it: allowing
  duplication buys the `reduce`, `chain` and `compose` classes at the cost of a
  ρ floor that rises with the overlap. See §9 of the finding document.
- **SPEC §11.5's ρ < 2.0** is unattainable on every corpus measured. The claim
  needs revising, not another sweep.
- **Two GitHub personal access tokens must be revoked** — prefixes `ghp_OrpLEW…`
  and `ghp_cX4VRh…`. **Checked on 4 September and this entry is narrower than it
  was:** the repositories are already archived and the remotes are already
  clean. All three working copies — `Swarmbly-AI_Clean` and the two under
  `_archive/` — point at `https://github.com/Sebastardito/Swarmbly-AI.git` with
  no embedded credential, and no `.git/config`, `.git-credentials` or `.netrc`
  under `P2PAI` holds the string. What remains is the revocation itself, on
  github.com → Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens. No run
  and no commit does that.

  The full values are deliberately **not** written here, and this file was
  checked rather than trusted: the only `ghp_` strings left in the tree are 8
  and 10 characters — prefixes. A note recording a live credential in a
  committed repository is a second copy of the problem.
